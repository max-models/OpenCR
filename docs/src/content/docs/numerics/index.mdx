OpenCR is a zero-dimensional collisional-radiative (CR) model of a low-temperature plasma: the
populations of explicit states (atomic levels, ions, molecular levels) coupled by elementary
reactions with traceable data. From them come the quasi-static populations of the excited
states for given driver densities, the effective ionization, recombination and photon
emissivity coefficients, closed steady states, the time evolution and the line emission. The
electron temperature or energy distribution, the electron density (or quasi-neutrality), the
heavy-particle temperatures and the escape factors are inputs: no energy equation and no
transport are solved.

Each equation on these pages is declared on the code that implements it, as a method card, and
checked by the tests: 23 closed forms against SymPy rules, four discretisations against their
order of convergence. The pages of the section list the cards module by module; this one shows
how they fit together, for the predefined atomic hydrogen model (`hydrogen/atomic/basic`).

## The pipeline

1. **[Population equations](/OpenCR/numerics/kinetics-system/).** Reaction $r$ proceeds at
   $w_r = k_r n_e^{a_{re}} \prod_j n_j^{a_{rj}}$ (mass action), and every state changes by
   $dn_i/dt = \sum_r \nu_{ir} w_r$. The electron is never a state: $n_e$ is given or
   quasi-neutral. The Jacobian is analytic and sparse; the conserved quantities are the
   elements.
2. **[Rate models](/OpenCR/numerics/core-rates/)** say how each $k_r$ is given: a cross
   section, Arrhenius, a table, a constant (Einstein $A$), a Python function, or the
   detailed-balance inverse of another reaction.
3. **[Rate coefficients](/OpenCR/numerics/kinetics-rates/).** A piecewise-linear cross section
   is averaged over a Maxwellian in closed form, segment by segment, with the threshold taken
   out so that the inverses never overflow. De-excitation and three-body recombination follow
   by Boltzmann and Saha, or by the Klein-Rosseland cross section for any
   [electron energy distribution](/OpenCR/numerics/kinetics-eedf/):

<MethodCard name="opencr.kinetics.rates.RateEvaluator.coefficients" headingLevel={3} />

4. **[Quasi-static reduction](/OpenCR/numerics/kinetics-effective/).** With the drivers
   (H(n=1) and H$^+$) prescribed, the excited states follow them instantly; eliminating them
   gives the population coefficients and the effective (CR) rate coefficients
   $S_{\mathrm{CR}}$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{CR}}$:

<MethodCard name="opencr.kinetics.effective.quasi_static" headingLevel={3} />

5. **[Steady states](/OpenCR/numerics/solvers-steady/)** solve $R(\mathbf n) = 0$: a sparse LU
   solve for a linear system, one row per conserved element replaced by its total for a closed
   one, Brent's method in $\ln n_e$ for quasi-neutrality, and pseudo-transient continuation for
   the rest, judged by the [relative residual](/OpenCR/numerics/solvers-diagnostics/).
   **[Time integration](/OpenCR/numerics/solvers-transient/)** uses SciPy's stiff integrators
   with the analytic Jacobian.
6. **[Radiation trapping](/OpenCR/numerics/kinetics-radiation/).** An optically thick line is
   reabsorbed: its $A$ is multiplied by an escape factor of the line-centre optical depth of a
   Doppler-broadened line, iterated with the populations to
   [self-consistency](/OpenCR/numerics/model/).
7. **[Emission](/OpenCR/numerics/analysis-emission/):** the lines, their
   [photon emissivity coefficients](/OpenCR/numerics/analysis-results/) per driver, Doppler and
   instrumental widths, the [electron cooling](/OpenCR/numerics/analysis-quantities/)
   coefficient.

The **[atomic hydrogen model](/OpenCR/numerics/models-hydrogen-atomic/)** puts the
[hydrogen data](/OpenCR/numerics/data-hydrogen/) together: levels resolved in $n$, the
Janev-Reiter-Samm and Johnson cross sections (from plasmacoll), Vriens-Smeets rates above
$n = 24$, Johnson's Einstein coefficients and the radiative recombination fits. Around the
model: the [sensitivities](/OpenCR/numerics/inference-sensitivity/) to the rate data and the
propagation of their uncertainties, the
[effective-rate tables](/OpenCR/numerics/tables/) other codes interpolate, and the
[batched](/OpenCR/numerics/backends-batched/) and [JAX](/OpenCR/numerics/backends-jax-backend/)
reductions of many conditions at once.

## Units and conventions

SI units, except energies and temperatures ($kT$) in eV; densities in m$^{-3}$, a rate
coefficient of a reaction with $k$ reactants in m$^{3(k-1)}$/s, wavelengths in nm in vacuum
(in [air](/OpenCR/numerics/constants/) above 200 nm on request). The constants are CODATA 2018
through `scipy.constants`, and $I_H = 13.598434599702$ eV from the NIST ASD. The hydrogen cross
sections keep the report's 13.6 eV thresholds while the states use $I_H$: the 0.01% difference
enters the detailed-balance exponents.

## Not in the model

- No energy equation, no EEDF calculation, no transport beyond explicit wall-loss and pumping
  reactions: zero-dimensional.
- Atomic hydrogen: $l$ sub-states statistically populated (overestimates 2s at low density); no
  levels above $n_{\max}$, no continuum lowering; electron collisions only (no proton or atom
  impact, no $l$-mixing); no two-photon decay of 2s, fine structure or Lamb shift; Kramers
  recombination without Gaunt factor for $n \ge 3$; Vriens-Smeets rates Maxwellian only.
- Radiation: optically thin unless escape factors are given or computed; trapping is an
  escape-factor approximation over a user length, without stimulated emission, redistribution
  or continuum opacity; spectra are Gaussian lines only.
- Detailed balance for inverses with different numbers of heavy particles (dissociation,
  attachment), or Saha inverses of non-electron rates.
- The reduction, the batched and the JAX paths need one heavy reactant per reaction and a given
  $n_e$.

## Verification

Besides the checks of the cards, the tests compare a closed steady state without radiation with
Saha-Boltzmann to $10^{-8}$, the Maxwellian closed form with plasmacoll's quadrature to 0.2%,
the corona limit, the two-level reduction and the Jacobian with finite differences, the Einstein
coefficients with NIST and the radiative recombination with Osterbrock and Ferland, and
$S_{\mathrm{CR}}$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{CR}}$ with OPEN-ADAS when its files are available; see
the [verification guide](/OpenCR/guides/verification/).
